Understanding People

The SCARF Model

Why people react the way they do, and what to do about it

🧠

Developed by Dr David Rock, SCARF names five things our brains treat as rewards or threats: Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness. Read the reaction, and you can usually spot which one is in play.

The Core Mindset
🎯
People rarely react to the words. They react to the threat or reward underneath them.

A client who goes quiet after a price, a colleague who bristles at a rota change, a first-timer who keeps asking "are you sure?" - these aren't random. Each one is a signal that one of five needs has just been touched. SCARF gives you the "why" behind the behaviour, so you can respond to the real thing instead of just the surface complaint.

Our Values, Through the SCARF Lens
PASSION
Genuine enthusiasm is one of the fastest ways to build Relatedness with a client.
PEOPLE
Reading the person first is Status and Relatedness in action, before you say a word.
PROFESSIONALISM
Honest indications and kept commitments protect Certainty and Fairness.
PRIDE

PURPOSE

SStatus Pride
🏅
Everyone wants to feel recognised, competent, and taken seriously.

Being corrected in front of others, made to feel unsure, or quietly talked down to, all sting more than the words themselves suggest. This shows up with clients who feel judged for not knowing travel jargon, and with colleagues who feel overlooked after putting in the work.

🧳With Clients
Watch For
  • Over-explaining basics, as if apologising for asking
  • Going quiet after being corrected on a fact
  • Comparing themselves to "people who know what they're doing"
Protect It
  • Credit what they already know before adding to it
  • Never make a correction sound like a mistake on their part
Try"You've actually done more research than most people who come to me, let me just fill in the gaps."
👥With the Team
Watch For
  • A consultant who goes quiet in group feedback
  • Defensiveness when a booking is queried
  • Pride in a hard-won client relationship being dismissed
Protect It
  • Give feedback on the work, never the person, and do it privately first
  • Name what someone did well before what needs to change
Try"That was a genuinely tricky client, here's one thing that would make it even stronger next time."
CCertainty Professionalism
🧭
Being able to predict what happens next feels safe. Not knowing feels unsafe, even when nothing has actually gone wrong yet.

Travel is full of moments where certainty is naturally low, prices not released, availability unconfirmed, a supplier gone quiet. The anxiety a client feels isn't really about the missing information, it's about not knowing what happens next.

✅ Builds Certainty
  • Naming what you don't know yet, and when you will know it
  • Giving a realistic timeframe and then honouring it
  • Explaining the next step, even if the outcome is still open
❌ Erodes Certainty
  • "Leave it with me" with no timeframe attached
  • Going quiet mid-process without an update
  • Changing a plan without explaining why
AAutonomy Purpose
🎛️
People want a say in how something happens to them, even in small ways.

A client who feels steered rather than guided will resist, even toward a genuinely good option. A colleague who is simply told a rota or process has changed, with no input sought, will disengage faster than one who was consulted, even if the outcome is identical.

🧳With Clients
Watch For
  • Pushback on a "recommended" option, even a strong one
  • Requests to "just see everything" rather than a shortlist
Try"Here are two strong options, both work well, which direction feels more like you?"
👥With the Team
Watch For
  • Resistance to a new process announced with no context
  • "Why are we doing it this way now" pushback
Try"Here's the change and why, what would make this easier to work with day to day?"
RRelatedness People
🤝
We trust people we feel some connection with, and we're wary of people who feel purely transactional.

This is why a chat that reads as "order-taking" loses clients even when the information given is accurate, and why a supplier who has only ever heard from us when something is wrong will be slower to help when we need it.

⚠️

Relatedness cannot be faked. A scripted "how are you today" lands as transactional, not warm, if it isn't followed by actually listening to the answer. The connection has to be genuine, even if it's brief.

FFairness Professionalism
⚖️
People will accept a difficult outcome if it feels fair. They will fight a small one if it doesn't.

A client who feels a promise was broken, or a colleague who feels a rule was applied to them but not someone else, reacts far harder than the situation alone would predict. Fairness is often the real story behind a complaint that seems to be "about" something smaller.

✅ Builds Fairness
  • Applying the same standard to everyone, consistently
  • Explaining the reasoning behind a decision, not just the decision
  • Owning a broken commitment quickly, before it's raised
❌ Erodes Fairness
  • Bending a rule quietly for one client or colleague
  • Letting a promise slip without acknowledging it
  • Defending a decision without explaining the "why"
Reading the Signal
🔎
When a reaction feels bigger than the situation, ask which S-C-A-R-F domain is actually being threatened.

A frustrated client isn't always frustrated about the thing they're saying. A defensive colleague isn't always defending the point being made. Naming the real domain, even just to yourself, changes how you respond, and usually de-escalates faster than addressing the surface issue alone.

The one rule: when a reaction seems out of proportion, it usually isn't about the words on the surface. Pause, name the SCARF domain likely being touched, and respond to that, not just the complaint as stated. This is a diagnostic lens, not a script, use it alongside our other guides to understand the "why" behind what you're already trained to do.
