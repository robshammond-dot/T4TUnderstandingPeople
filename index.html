Understanding People

The SCARF Model

Why clients react the way they do, and what to do about it

🧠

Developed by Dr David Rock, SCARF names five things our brains treat as rewards or threats: Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness. Read the client's reaction, and you can usually spot which one is in play.

The Core Mindset
🎯
Clients rarely react to the words. They react to the threat or reward underneath them.

A client who goes quiet after a price, a first-timer who keeps asking "are you sure?", a regular who suddenly feels short with you. These aren't random. Each one is a signal that one of five needs has just been touched. SCARF gives you the "why" behind the behaviour, so you can respond to what the client actually needs, not just the question they typed.

SStatus Pride
🏅
Every client wants to feel respected, capable, and taken seriously.

Clients who don't know travel jargon, or who are booking their first trip abroad, can feel judged very easily. Being corrected, rushed, or answered with terms they don't understand stings more than the words themselves suggest.

👀Watch For
  • Over-explaining basics, as if apologising for asking
  • Going quiet after being corrected on a fact
  • Comparing themselves to "people who know what they're doing"
  • Mentioning a budget apologetically, or not at all
🛡️Protect It
  • Credit what they already know before adding to it
  • Never make a correction sound like a mistake on their part
  • Swap jargon for plain words, without pointing out that you did
  • Treat every budget as a real budget for a real holiday
Try"You've actually done more research than most people who come to me, let me just fill in the gaps."
CCertainty Professionalism
🧭
Being able to predict what happens next feels safe. Not knowing feels unsafe, even when nothing has actually gone wrong yet.

Travel is full of moments where certainty is naturally low: prices not released, availability unconfirmed, a supplier still to respond. The anxiety a client feels isn't really about the missing information, it's about not knowing what happens next.

👀Watch For
  • "Just checking in" messages soon after an enquiry
  • Asking the same question in different ways
  • Nervousness about documents, visas or what to pack
🛡️Protect It
  • Name what you don't know yet, and when you will know it
  • Give a realistic timeframe, then honour it
  • Explain the next step, even when the outcome is still open
Try"I'm waiting on the hotel to confirm. I'll update you by tomorrow afternoon, even if I'm still waiting, so you're never left wondering."
✅ Builds Certainty
  • A clear timeframe with every "leave it with me"
  • Proactive updates, even when there's no news yet
  • Walking through what happens from booking to departure
❌ Erodes Certainty
  • "Leave it with me" with no timeframe attached
  • Going quiet mid-process without an update
  • Changing a plan without explaining why
AAutonomy Purpose
🎛️
Clients want a say in their own holiday, even in small ways.

A client who feels steered rather than guided will resist, even toward a genuinely good option. The same recommendation lands very differently when it's offered as a choice.

👀Watch For
  • Pushback on a "recommended" option, even a strong one
  • Requests to "just see everything" rather than a shortlist
  • "I'll think about it" straight after a firm recommendation
🛡️Protect It
  • Offer two or three good options, and let them choose
  • Share your view as a recommendation, not a decision
  • Ask what matters most to them before narrowing down
Try"Here are two strong options, both work well. Which direction feels more like you?"
RRelatedness People
🤝
Clients trust people they feel some connection with, and they're wary of anyone who feels purely transactional.

This is why a chat that reads as "order-taking" can lose a client even when every piece of information given is accurate. The connection doesn't need to be long, it needs to be real.

👀Watch For
  • Short, one-word replies that feel guarded
  • A client who shares why they're travelling (a birthday, a honeymoon, a first trip)
  • Asking for "the same person as last time"
🛡️Protect It
  • Pick up on the reason for the trip and use it
  • Use their name, and yours
  • Show genuine interest in the trip, not just the booking
Try"A 40th in Zanzibar, what a way to celebrate! Let's make sure the hotel knows it's a special one."
⚠️

Relatedness cannot be faked. A scripted "how are you today" lands as transactional, not warm, if it isn't followed by actually listening to the answer. The connection has to be genuine, even if it's brief.

FFairness Professionalism
⚖️
Clients will accept a difficult outcome if it feels fair. They will fight a small one if it doesn't.

A client who feels a promise was broken, or that they were given less than someone else, reacts far harder than the situation alone would predict. Fairness is often the real story behind a complaint that seems to be "about" something smaller.

✅ Builds Fairness
  • Explaining the reason behind a price or a policy, not just stating it
  • Being upfront about what is and isn't included
  • Owning a broken commitment quickly, before the client raises it
❌ Erodes Fairness
  • A price that changes with no explanation
  • Letting a promise slip without acknowledging it
  • "That's just the policy" with nothing behind it

Reading the Signal
🔎
When a reaction feels bigger than the situation, ask which S-C-A-R-F need is actually being touched.

A frustrated client isn't always frustrated about the thing they're saying. Naming the real need, even just to yourself, changes how you respond, and usually settles things faster than answering the surface question alone.

The one rule: when a client's reaction seems out of proportion, it usually isn't about the words on the surface. Pause, name the SCARF need likely being touched, and respond to that, not just the question as stated. This is a way of understanding clients, not a script. Use it alongside our other guides to understand the "why" behind what you're already trained to do.
